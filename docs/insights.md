# Key Findings (synthetic data, cut-off 31 Dec 2025)

Figures come from `analysis/kpi_summary.py`; the Power BI report must reconcile to these.

| KPI | Value |
|---|---|
| Total spend | EUR 27.6M (2024: 13.4M, 2025: 14.2M, +5.5% YoY) |
| Contract compliance | 87.1% (maverick spend 12.9%) |
| Price variance vs contract | +EUR 0.72M (+2.7%) |
| On-time / In-full / OTIF | 79.9% / 91.5% / 73.8% |
| Rejection rate | 3.3% |
| Paid on time | 71.9% |
| Top 10 supplier share | 48.0% (30 of 60 suppliers make 80% of spend) |
| Stock-out item-months | 12.5% (32.6% below reorder level) |

## What the analysis shows
1. **Delivery is the weakest link.** OTIF is 73.8%. Three suppliers (Harbour Trading, Ironbridge Supplies, Ironbridge Industrial) deliver OTIF below 50%; Harbour Trading has 552 delivered lines, so its poor performance has real operational impact.
2. **Quality issues are concentrated.** Three suppliers reject more than 10% of units (Liffey Distribution, Summit Supplies, Harbour Components) against a 3.3% average.
3. **Maverick spend is a department behaviour problem.** Projects (22.9%) and Sales (17.6%) buy off-contract most; Finance, IT and Health & Safety are below 4%. Off-contract buys carry a price premium, so compliance training has a direct savings value.
4. **Payment discipline.** Only 71.9% of invoices are paid by the due date, risking late-payment friction with suppliers.
5. **Stock risk.** One in eight item-months ends at zero stock, largely on items from low-OTIF suppliers.

## Recommendations
- Place the lowest-OTIF suppliers on a performance improvement plan and qualify an alternate source for their top items.
- Introduce a catalogue/PO gate for Projects and Sales to cut off-contract purchasing.
- Raise safety stock on items supplied by suppliers with OTIF below 60%.
- Review payment run frequency to lift on-time payment above 90%.
